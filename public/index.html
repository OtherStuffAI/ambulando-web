Think together. Then move.

You don’t need the whole plan
to take the first step.

Start with the messy version. Explore a topic with your agent, let it learn how you work, and shape the structure together when you’re ready.

  1. 01 / EXPLORE

    Say what’s on your mind.

    “Our client handovers keep slipping.” Talk through what happens, what gets missed and what a good outcome looks like. Bring the files and examples you already use.

  2. 02 / UNDERSTAND

    Hear it played back.

    Your agent asks about the exceptions, plays the process back and builds walkthroughs or whiteboards to make it clear. Correct it together until the brief matches your goal, then agree on the next step.

  3. 03 / CAPTURE

    Give the work a home.

    Keep the agreed brief, decisions and context in a document. Capture the work as tasks and a workflow so the goal and responsibilities have a home beyond your memory.

  4. 04 / DELIVER

    Make progress you can inspect.

    Agents build the apps and infrastructure, work through connected tools and record their progress on the tasks. Follow the updates, spot blockers and inspect the evidence. Make the decisions that need you, then carry what you learned into the next round.

CLEAR RESPONSIBILITY

Different roles, one team.

A designer shares a direction. A research agent gathers evidence. A coding agent builds the change. The owner reviews it. The task preserves who is responsible and what’s ready.

YOUR BOUNDARIES

Share what is needed.

Give a contractor access to a client project, keep private operations separate and require approval before publishing. Identity, permissions and workflow rules define who may act.

Access follows the permissions you configure. Being connected or online does not give an agent permission to publish, spend or see everything.

Before you step in

A few useful answers.

How do Ambulando, NStack and Wingman fit together?

Ambulando is the human workspace. NStack is the shared infrastructure for data, files, knowledge and access. Wingman is an agent execution system that can connect to them, run workflows and host specialised apps. Everything plugs into NStack so you can own your stack.

How do I get started with NStack?

Start in Ambulando ↗. The intended onboarding is to sign up for your NStack in the app and choose hosted or your own infrastructure. Provider availability and automated setup depend on the supported installation; this proposal does not establish that all provisioning paths are live.

Do I have to use Wingman?

You can work with other agents through skills and CLIs. Wingman is especially designed to work with NStack and Ambulando, and Autopilot can run your existing agents with a graphical UI and workflow support. Each agent still needs supported tools and the appropriate permissions.

How do I keep control of what an agent can do?

Configure access for the people, agents and resources involved. Put review and approval boundaries into the workflow, especially before publishing, spending or changing important systems. Test the permissions and failure paths for your actual setup.

Are the examples ready-made products?

The screenshots show the actual Ambulando interface with sample data. The business examples explain processes you could build using the connected system. They are not a catalogue of installed apps, and a recurring workflow still needs validation, monitoring and maintenance.
